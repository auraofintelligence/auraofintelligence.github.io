Grey Area Commons

A calm, private doorway into the human questions most places won't touch. Not to make anyone uncomfortable, but because avoiding them helps no one: this is a gentle, consent-first way to think about connection, before you ever share a word of it with another person.

What this is

The grey area between private and shared

Grey Area Commons is an adults-only, privacy-preserving question path for making loving, intimate connection feel less anxious, more consensual, and easier to move through gently. It starts soft, with ease-in questions, and lets you go only as far as you choose, into loving-connection questions, the sharing layers you set yourself, and gentle AI support that helps you find the words.

It is honest about what it holds. Human relationships and sexuality are not one shape: they span cultures, structures and kinds, from the quietly ordinary to non-traditional forms like group marriages, and it is more responsible to make a safe, consent-first place to think about all of it than to pretend none of it exists. This matters right now, when loneliness, mental health and falling birth rates are real, and connection is genuinely hard for a lot of people. There is even a gentle branch for memory, love and intimacy where a family history of memory decline is part of the picture, which is why it sits close to the Anti-Dementia work.

The privacy is the point. It runs entirely in your browser, keeps your answers only for your current session, and lets you export plain Markdown you control and then clear. Nothing is sent anywhere, nothing is published for you, and nothing here is consent, or legal or medical advice: it is a private draft, for your own reflection, that you decide what to do with.

Carry it forward, wherever you are

A consent-first commons of your own

Underneath the questions is a pattern you can build on: a way for a person, or a whole community, to hold the tender conversations privately and consensually, on their own terms and in their own culture. Take it as a worked example and shape your own, in the language and customs that fit your people, with the same two rules that make it safe: consent comes first, and privacy is the default, never the afterthought.

That's the whole point of building your own tools instead of renting someone else's, and it matters most exactly here, where what you're holding is the most personal thing there is. You keep what you make, no one else sees it unless you decide, and the short video walks through how to approach it gently. Take what fits, leave the rest.
